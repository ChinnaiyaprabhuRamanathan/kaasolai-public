## Returns ,

- **Absolute Returns** – Returns without considering number of years
- **CAGR** – Compounded Annual Growth Rate CAGR. Return for each year (one time investment and withdrawal). Use CAGR calculator online with inputs: initial amount, final amount, number of years.
- **XIRR** – Extended Internal Rate of Return XIRR. Return for each year (irregular investment withdrawal). Use Excel XIRR formula
- **Rolling Returns** – Return calculated unlike point to point CAGR. Like 365 CAGRs returns calculated then averaged
- **ROI, Return On Investment** – (Profit from Investment / CostOfInvestment)\*100

---

## Some other metrics

- **α – Alpha** : Percentage of return greater than benchmark. Baseline : 0 . Above 0 is good, below 0 is bad
- **β – Beta** : Volatility based on bench mark. Baseline : 1 . Above is bad, below is good.
- **σ – Standard Deviation** : Deviation/Fluctuation of return from average. Baseline : average. Above avg is bad, below is good.
- **Sharp Ratio** : Returns with respect to Risk. more the better

## ***Financial Statements Analysis ,***

There are 3 financial statements :

1. Income Statement / P&L Statement
2. Balance Sheet
3. Cash Flow Statement

---

## 1. The Income Statement ,

The Flow from top line to bottom line,

- Sales Revenue
- – COGS
- Gross Profit
- – OPEX excluding D&A
- EBITDA
- – D&A
- Operating Profit / EBIT
- ± Non Operational Income & Expenses
- PreTax Income / EBT
- – Tax
- Net Profit / Income / PAT

Lets go one by one,

- **Sale Revenue** – Total Revenue made by the company
- **COGS**, Cost Of Goods Sold – Making Cost of the product like raw materials
- **Gross Profit** – Profit left after COGS
- **OPEX**, Operating Expenditure – Cost for operating the company like labour salary, electricity bills. Depreciation & Amortisation are also Operating costs. D&A are non cash expenses for assets to account for reduction in value over time. Depreciation is for tangible assets like furniture, car. Amortisation is for intangible assets like ip rights
- **Operating Profit / EBIT**, Earnings Before Interest and Tax – Profit left until operating expenses. Still Interest and Tax are not accounted.
- **Non Operational Income & Expenses** – It includes cash activities that does not come under operational activity of the business. These include interest paid or received, cash received from sale of asset.
- **PreTax Income / EBT**, Earnings Before Tax – Profit left until all expenses are accounted except Tax.
- **Tax** – Tax paid to government. Can be deferred or paid early.
- **Net Profit / Income** **/ PAT, Profit After Tax** – The money left at last.

---

CapEx – Capital Expenditure : Funds used by company for PPE. Also associated with expansion.

---

Checkout these profitability ratios from P&L,

- **GPM** , Gross Profit Margin
  - GPM = Gross Profit / Sales
  - How much profit is left after COGS
- **OPM** , Operating Profit Margin
  - OPM = Operating Profit / Sales
  - How much profit is left until OPEX
- **NPM** , Net Profit Margin
  - NPM = Net Income / Sales
  - How much profit is left at last
- **EPS** , Earnings Per Share
  - EPS = ( Net Income – Preferred Dividend ) / OutStanding Shares
  - How much is one share earning

---

## 2. The Balance Sheet

There are 3 sections in a balance sheet

1. Assests
2. Liability
3. Equity

**Assets** : What we own . It can be Current Asset or LongTerm/Fixed Asset . Also, operating Asset or non-operating asset.

**Liability** : What we owe . Debt is a liability . It can be Current/ShortTerm or LongTerm Liability. ShortTerm Liability is for operating activities like accounts payable. LongTerm Liability are debts and bonds. LongTerm Liabilities are interest bearing in nature.

**Equity :** Assets after all liabilities are deducted

---

The Accounting Equation** : Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

Net Working Capital = Current Assets – Current Liabilities

**WACC** – Weighted Average Cost of Capital

- WACC = Cost of Equity + Cost of Debt after tax. Cost of debt is considered after tax because interest expenses on debt are tax deductible.

- Insight : The average rate of return a company must pay to its investors to finance its assets. Helps understand the cost of the capital. Lower WACC indicates cheaper financing. Projects with higher return than WACC is value adding to the company.

---

Checkout these profitability ratios from Balance Sheet,

- **ROA**, Return on Asset
  - NetIncome / Average Total Asset
- **ROE**, Return on Equity
  - NetIncome / Shareholders Equity
  - Also have a look at ROE breakdown using DuPont Analysis
- **ROCE**, Return on Capital Employed
  - Operating Profit / Capital Employed
  - Capital Employed = Equity + LongTerm Liabilities
  - Or Capital Employed = Total Asset – Current Liabilities
- **ROIC**, Return on Invested Capital
  - NOPAT / Invested Capital
  - NOPAT, Net Operating Profit After Tax
  - NOPAT = Operating Profit \* (1-TaxRate)
  - Invested Capital = Interest bearing Liability + Equity – Non Operating Assets
  - Or Invested Capital = Debt + Equity + Minority Interest – Excess Cash.
  - Or Invested Capital = PPE + NonCurrent Operating Asset + Net Working Capital .
  - PPE is Property, Plant, and Equipment, i.e., Long Term tangible Asset used in Operation
  - Also for a company to create value ROIC > WACC, Weighted Average Cost of Capital.

---

Checkout these Turnover ratios from Balance Sheet,

- **Inventory Turnover Ratio**
  - COGS / Average Inventory
  - Sales include profit margin and therefore COGS is used instead of Sales
  - How many times the company replaces its inventory
  - The more the better
- **Asset Turnover Ratio**
  - Sales / Average Total Assets
  - Amount of Sales generated per rupee of the assets
  - The more the better
- **Accounts Payable Turnover Ratio** / Creditors Turnover Ratio
  - Creditor – Lender
  - Company is a borrower
  - Supplier Credit Purchase / Average accounts payable
  - How many times the company pays off its suppliers
  - The more means liquidity, less means missed opportunity
- **Receivables Turnover Ratio** / Debt Turnover Ratio
  - Debtor – Borrower
  - Company is a lender
  - Credit Sales / Average accounts receivables
  - How many times a company collects its receivables
  - The more the better

---

Checkout these Solvency ratios from Balance Sheet,

- **Debt to Equity Ratio**
  - Total Liabilities / Total Shareholders Equity
  - Company’s reliance on debt than equity in a long run
  - Measure of Companies financial leverage
  - How much rupee in debt is the company having for a 1 rupee of Equity
- **Interest Coverage Ratio** / TIE, Times Interest Earned Ratio
  - EBIT / Interest expense
  - Company’s ability to pay interest obligations with its earnings
  - Must be higher
- **Debt to Asset Ratio**
  - Total Debt / Total Asset
  - Company’s Assets that are financed by debt
  - Must be less than 1. Greater than 1 means the company is insolvent
- **Equity Ratio**
  - Total Shareholders Equity / Total Assets
  - Company’s Assets that are financed by Equity
  - 1 means company funded completely by equity. lower means reliance of debt. Mostly expressed in %

---

Checkout these Liquidity ratios from Balance Sheet,

- **Current Ratio**
  - Current Asset / Current Liability
  - Company’s ability to pay short-term obligations
  - Greater than 1 is better
- **Quick Ratio**
  - Quick Asset / Current Liability
  - Quick Asset = Current Asset – Inventory – Prepaid Expenses
  - Quick Asset = Cash&CashEquivalent + Marketable Securities + Net Accounts Receivables
  - Company’s ability to pay short-term obligations with most liquid assets
  - Greater than 1 is better
- **Cash Ratio**
  - Cash&CashEquivalent / Current Liability
  - Company’s ability to pay short-term obligations with just cash&cashEquivalents
  - Less than 1 is ok , More than 1 is inefficiency

## 3. Cash Flow Statement

Shows the actual cash transactions in a company without accrual accounting.

Involves 3 sections and each sections had cash inflows and cash outflows,

1. Operating Activities
2. Investing Activities
3. Financing Activities

The companies Net Cash Flow must be positive. Positive Cash flow means excess cash generated, indicates potential expansion. Negative Cash flow means more cash burned than generated, can also indicates ongoing expansion. Persistent Negative Cash flow without a clear strategy for turning it positive is a alarming red flag.

- **Operating Activities** – Cash generated from a company’s core business operations. Includes : Taxes, D&A

- **Investing Activities** – Cash generated from a company’s Investment activities. Includes : Purchase/Sales of Assets

- **Financing Activities** – Cash generated from a company’s financing activities. Includes : Cash activities from debt and equity.

---

Checkout these CashFlow Metrics from CashFlow Statement,

- **CashFlow / CFO** – Cash Flow from Operations
  - Insight : Company’s ability to generate cash from its core business. Cash left for expansion
  - CFO = Net Income + NonCashExpenses – WorkingCapital
  - Directly Taken from CashFlowStatement’s Operating Activity
- **FCF** – Free Cash Flow
  - Insight : Cash left after expansion
  - FCF = CFO – CapEx
- **FCFE** – Free Cash Flow to Equity / LFCF – Levered FCF
  - Insight : Cash left for shareholders after debt repayments
  - FCFE = FCF + Net Borrowing
- **FCFF** – Free Cash Flow to Firm / UFCF – UnLevered FCF
  - Insight : Cash left for all investors before any interests payments are made.
  - FCFF = NOPAT + NonCashExpenses – WorkingCapital – CapEx
  - Gives EV of a business. Interest is not considered as it is only for debt investors.

## 1. Relative Valuation

- P/E Ratio
- PEG Ratio
- P/B Ratio
  - PB Ratio – Market price per share / Book value per share. P/B < 1 means undervalued, P/B > 1 means overvalued
- P/S Ratio
- EV/EBITDA Ratio
- P/FCFF Ratio
